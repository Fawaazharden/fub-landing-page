The gap

A 30 day audit of one client’s CRM found 5 of 312 recent leads with any property activity on the record.

That was not a lazy team. Eight licensed agents, a real CRM, a paid AI tool already running, and leads arriving from paid traffic every day. It is just what follow up looks like when it depends on a human having a free minute at the exact moment a stranger decides to reply.

01

The first reply decides it

Nobody fills in one form. A buyer poking around at 9pm fills in three or four inside ten minutes, and the team that gets a human reply back is usually the one still in the conversation a week later. Your agent was at dinner.

02

Agents work the leads who answer. That is rational and it is also why the rest of the pipeline quietly dies. Nobody decides to abandon a lead. It just never gets touched again.

Why each of those is deliberate

The first message says who and why

A client read an early version and said it had “no context”. He was right. Nothing in the prompt had ever required a message to make sense to somebody who does not remember us. Now every message names the sender, the reason it is arriving, and asks one thing.

The reply waits 25 to 35 seconds

The same client wrote: “Speed in which the AI responds via text. Its so fast it can feel bot like.” The pause is measured from the lead’s message rather than added after drafting, so every reply lands in the same window no matter how fast the model was.

The link is its own message

A long search URL stacked onto prose pushes the message across several SMS segments. Handsets reassemble that badly and the one part with a job stops being clickable. So the prose lands first, then the link.

It asks before it decides

Sending listings without asking is the AI deciding for the lead that they did not want a person. So it asks once. Yes routes to an agent. No gets the listings and a soft landing. Silence gets the listings anyway after 90 minutes, because a lead left holding a question is worse than either.

The part nobody shows you

Five things this system got wrong before a single real lead saw it.

Most of these were found by the client himself, running his own scenarios against a test contact he created, which is exactly what acceptance testing is supposed to look like. All of them were fixed before any real lead was in scope. We publish them because a defect list is the only honest way to show what an implementation actually involves, and because the vendor who tells you nothing ever goes wrong is the one to worry about.

The AI invented an answer from its own voicemail

On an unanswered call the transcript is the machine greeting plus our own message. The post call analysis read our words back as the lead’s answer, decided they had told us their area, and wrote it onto the contact record. Worse than the wrong data: a question that reads as answered is never asked again.

Fixed by merging facts only from a call that actually connected, and by teaching the analysis that the agent’s own words are never evidence.
A brush off was logged as an interested lead

A 22 second call whose entire content was “is now an ok time?” and “not really” was written to the CRM with the outcome Interested. That is the field agents filter on to decide who to ring back, so the damage lands on a human wasting an afternoon.

Fixed by requiring evidence of interest before that outcome can be written. Bad timing now has its own signal.
“Now is not a good time” was recorded as refusing an agent

The lead got a tag and a note saying they had turned down a live transfer they were never offered, plus the never offer an agent again path. Bad timing is the most common answer an unscheduled call gets, so this was mislabelling the majority of first calls.

Fixed with a separate bad timing signal that writes no tag, claims no refusal, and leaves the door open.
One lead got the same introduction text three times in 1.7 seconds

Two schedulers raced each other. The step was marked done after the send rather than before it, so it sat marked pending for the few seconds the draft took, and the second scheduler picked it up and ran it again.

Fixed by claiming the step before anything else can touch it. The copy checker had actually flagged the duplicates and sent anyway, which is the right behaviour and no substitute for fixing the race.
A search link built from nothing

A lead who had answered no questions received a property link that resolved to eight ZIP codes across three counties, no price, no property type. The client’s own words were “a link to properties with not much context”. A link to everything is worse than no link.

Fixed so no link goes out unless the lead actually named an area, and so the message asks the one question that would let us keep the promise.

Control

8am to 9pm, a tag your staff can pull mid sequence, and a log of everything that got blocked.

Point an AI at a few thousand phone numbers and you have taken on a compliance problem, whether or not you were thinking of it that way. These are built in, because the ones you have to remember to switch on are the ones that get missed.

  • An 8am to 9pm contact window in the lead’s own local time, matching the federal calling window for telephone solicitations. A step that comes due outside it is deferred to the next opening, never cancelled and never fired late.
  • Per contact activation is a tag in Follow Up Boss. Remove it and the AI stops on that lead immediately, mid sequence. The tag is re read before every single outbound action, so a missed webhook can never cause a text to somebody who was deliberately switched off.
  • A global stop that halts conversations already in flight, not just new ones.
  • Opt outs and suppression lists applied across both channels, with the reply processed on receipt.
  • An event log of what fired, what was blocked, and why, so a disputed message has an answer.
The consequence we tell clients before they find it

A contact window means a lead arriving at 11pm gets their first message at 8am. Speed to lead on overnight arrivals is therefore hours, by design, and it will show up in your reporting. That is the rule we build to, and you should hear it from us instead of noticing it in a dashboard six weeks in.

The telephony account, the messaging registration, the model provider and the hosting are all registered to you. We build inside them. If you fire us tomorrow, nothing switches off and nothing has to be exported, because there is nowhere for it to be exported from.

Fit

This is the wrong purchase for a lot of teams. Probably worth knowing which.

A subscription costs you a card on file. This costs you meetings. Somebody on your side has to sit down and answer what happens when a lead comes in on a Sunday in a town two agents both cover. After that it costs nothing to keep. If you cannot spare the meetings, buy the subscription.

Say no if
  • You want to be live this week. Discovery, build and acceptance testing take longer than switching on a SaaS trial.
  • Nobody on your side can answer questions about routing rules and scripts. Those answers are the product.
  • You are looking for lead generation. This works the leads you already have and does not create new ones.
  • Your volume is a handful of leads a month. Raw usage will be cheap, but the build will not pay for itself quickly.
  • You want a vendor to own it so there is somebody to blame when it breaks. There will not be. The telephony account is in your name and the invoice comes to you.

Before you book

Nine questions we get asked on every one of these calls.

How is this different from Follow Up Boss Smart Messages and Action Plans?

As they work today, Smart Messages help an agent draft a reply faster, and Action Plans organise sequences, tasks, emails, tags and stages. Both still need a human in the loop at the moment a lead replies. This adds an autonomous layer that calls or texts, holds the conversation, captures qualification, keeps following up over weeks, and writes the result back into Follow Up Boss before a person is involved.

Will my agents have to learn another tool?

No, and that is the main design constraint. Agents stay in Follow Up Boss. Conversations, summaries, qualification fields, notes, tags and assignment all land on the person record they already open. Turning the AI on or off for one contact is a tag they apply in the CRM. The only separate screen is an operator dashboard for whoever holds the global controls, and most agents never see it.

What does it actually cost to run each month?

You pay your own vendors directly: the carrier for messages and call minutes, the model provider for the AI, and a small hosting bill. There is no platform fee from us. What that totals depends entirely on your volume and how much of it is voice, because calls cost meaningfully more than texts. We size it with you on the call rather than quoting a number that would be wrong.

Do we have to replace our Follow Up Boss phone number?

Usually not. Messaging and voice are mapped around your current setup, and any number porting requirement is identified during discovery rather than sprung on you after the build. What does need doing is proper messaging registration for the sending number, which we handle with you.

Can it work aged or imported leads?

Yes, where consent, brokerage policy, carrier rules, suppression lists and opt outs permit it. Aged and purchased lists carry obligations that fresh inbound leads do not, so eligible segments get defined and approved before anything sends. This is the part of a reactivation project that takes the longest and it is not the part to rush.

How long does an implementation take?

Discovery is quick, usually a call and a follow up. The build depends on scope, and acceptance testing depends on how fast your side can run scenarios with us. A texting only build on one lead source is a short project. Voice, territory routing, live transfer and reactivation across a full roster is not. We give you a real date after discovery, not before.

What happens if it says something wrong to one of my leads?

You remove the tag on that contact and the AI stops immediately, mid sequence, and a human takes it. For anything wider there is a global stop that halts conversations already in flight. Every message and call is on the record with a timestamp, so you can see exactly what was said rather than reconstructing it. We would rather you had the log than our reassurance.

What exactly do we own at the end?

The accounts, the API keys, the phone number and its registration, the configuration, the documentation, and the conversation data, because all of it lives in systems registered to you. You pay raw telephony, model and hosting costs directly rather than renting our software indefinitely. If you stop working with us, nothing switches off.

Is there a free pilot?

Sometimes. It depends on your lead volume, your CRM setup and whether we think we can prove something meaningful quickly, so it is a conversation on the call rather than a standing offer on a page. What is always free is the teardown itself and the written map of your lead flow that comes out of it.
